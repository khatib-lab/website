Scientific Mission

Our Research

We establish new concepts in heterogeneous catalysis to enable the sustainable and renewable production of chemicals with atomic and molecular level precision.

Research Focus Areas

What We Investigate

Our research couples nano and molecular catalyst synthesis with advanced characterization and reactor engineering, applied to sustainable chemical processes at industrial and energy scales.

01 — Core Focus

Methane Dehydroaromatization (MDA)

A sustainable, single-step reaction for the direct conversion of methane — from natural gas, shale gas, biogas, and landfill gas — to benzene and hydrogen: 6 CH₄ → C₆H₆ + 9 H₂. We develop novel zeolite-supported metal carbide catalysts (Mo/ZSM-5 promoted with Fe, Co, or Ni) with improved benzene selectivity and lower propensity to deactivation by coke deposition. We study the complete catalyst life cycle: activation, reaction, deactivation, and regeneration.

Mo Carbide Catalysts ZSM-5 Zeolites Bimetallic Promoters Reaction Kinetics

02

COx-free Hydrogen Production

Catalytic methane decomposition (CMD) directly converts methane to hydrogen and solid carbon, producing no CO or CO₂ byproducts. We develop novel materials — metal oxides, metal carbides, and supported metal nanoparticles — to catalyze this reaction efficiently and with long on-stream stability. This pathway supports the production of clean hydrogen as a renewable energy carrier without greenhouse gas emissions.

Clean Hydrogen Novel Catalyst Materials Carbon-free Routes

03

Membrane Reactor Engineering

Conventional packed-bed reactors for MDA suffer from thermodynamic limitations and non-uniform oxidant distribution. We engineer gradient-feed membrane reactors (GFMR) that uniformly distribute oxidant co-reactants (O₂ or CO₂) across the catalyst bed. This approach improves process thermodynamics, increases methane conversion, maintains high benzene selectivity, and extends catalyst lifetime — achieving benzene yield improvements of up to twofold compared to packed-bed configurations.

GFMR Design Process Thermodynamics Reactor Engineering

04

In Situ & Operando Characterization

To capture the real structural evolution of catalysts under working conditions, we use advanced in situ and operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) and high-resolution powder diffraction at national synchrotron facilities including Brookhaven National Laboratory (NSLS-II) and SLAC/SSRL. Combined with ex situ techniques, this gives us a realistic picture of the structural and chemical changes occurring in catalytic phases during reaction — an essential input for rational catalyst design.

XAS / XANES / EXAFS Powder Diffraction Synchrotron Facilities Operando Methods

05

CO₂ Upgrading & Utilization

Carbon dioxide serves as both a challenge and an opportunity. In the MDA context, CO₂ acts as an oxidant co-reactant in the membrane reactor, regenerating active Mo carbide phases and reducing coking. More broadly, we investigate catalytic strategies to convert CO₂ into useful chemical building blocks, supporting circular economy goals and greenhouse gas mitigation.

CO₂ Co-reactant Circular Economy Carbon Utilization

06

Catalyst Stability & Deactivation

Rapid deactivation by coke deposition is the primary barrier preventing industrial implementation of MDA. We study deactivation mechanisms at the molecular level, developing strategies for catalyst regeneration and for designing catalysts with inherently lower coke formation. We investigate how catalyst pretreatment, metal loading, and support properties influence the nature and quantity of carbon deposits over the entire catalyst life cycle.

Our Philosophy

Bridging Molecular Science & Engineering

The broader impact of our work is to enable green technologies and circular economies, and to educate the engineering workforce of the future. We combine rigorous experimentation with computational tools and industrial collaboration to translate fundamental insights into real-world impact.

ARC Appalachian Regional Commission

Establishing a Hydrogen Innovation Hub in Appalachia via Catalytic Methane Decomposition $1.3M · 2025

Virginia Tech has received a $1.3 million grant from the Appalachian Regional Commission to lead natural gas conversion research in Southwest Virginia. The goal is to establish a premier hydrogen innovation hub that leverages the region's abundant natural gas reserves (Marcellus Shale, Appalachian Basin) to produce turquoise hydrogen via catalytic methane decomposition — generating hydrogen and solid carbon without CO₂ emissions. The solid carbon byproduct can be repurposed into high-value nanofibers and nanotubes. The project also includes a K–12 workforce development component to build energy literacy and sustainable energy skills in rural Southwest Virginia communities.

Partners: Amy Price Azano (VT Center for Rural Education) · Wilson Shafer (Asbury University) · Robert Hart / Shepherd Chemical Company

DOE-EERE Dept. of Energy
Energy Efficiency

Designing a Methane Dehydroaromatization Process for Feedstock Flexibility and High On-Stream Time via Dynamic Kinetic and Thermodynamic Control

This project engineers an alternative conversion technology using a gradient-feed membrane reactor (GFMR) that delivers oxidant co-reactants into the reactor to improve thermodynamics and increase methane conversion while maintaining high benzene selectivity and preventing oxidative destruction of active sites. The integrated approach combines GFMR design, stable catalyst development, microkinetic evaluation, in situ/operando characterization, and techno-economic analysis at demonstration and production scales.

Collaborators: Texas Tech University (Khatib & Chen groups) · Idaho National Laboratory (Fushimi group) · Shepherd Chemical Company · Stanford Synchrotron Radiation Lightsource at SLAC (Bare)

NSF CAREER National Science Foundation

Tailoring the Synergy between Catalyst Design and Reaction Engineering for Direct Conversion of Methane to Aromatics

This CAREER project studies stable Mo-X/HZSM-5 catalysts (X = Fe, Co, Ni) while operating in a distributed-feed membrane reactor (DFMR) fed with O₂ or CO₂ co-reactants. The goal is to determine the role that Mo-X-C interactions play on reaction and deactivation pathways within a DFMR. We monitor structure, location, and evolution of Mo-X phases by in situ and operando experiments including X-ray absorption and high-resolution powder diffraction, providing the basis for the rational design of catalysts tailored for DFMR environments.

NSF National Science Foundation

Integrated Experimental and Theoretical Endeavor for Fundamental Understanding of Processes in Methane Dehydroaromatization

This project prepares and evaluates model catalysts with active metals existing only on either the outer surface or within the zeolite channels. Structure, location, and evolution of Mo species are monitored by in situ and operando techniques. Experimental data are combined with density functional theory (DFT) calculations to advance understanding of the structure-activity relationship and reaction pathways in the complete MDA catalytic cycle. Beyond the research, the project also develops a virtual reality (VR) technology module that allows K–12 students to immerse themselves in a catalyst structure and interact directly with dynamic 3-D molecular images.

Co-PI: Josh Howe, Texas Tech

Methods & Tools

Techniques & Facilities

We combine a broad suite of experimental techniques and leverage world-class synchrotron facilities for our structural studies.

⚗

Catalyst Synthesis

Impregnation, ion-exchange, atomic layer deposition (ALD), and co-precipitation for preparing well-defined supported metal oxide, carbide, and bimetallic catalysts.

◉

X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy

XANES and EXAFS at Brookhaven (NSLS-II) and SLAC (SSRL) synchrotrons — in situ and operando — to probe electronic structure and local coordination of active metal species.

⊞

X-ray Diffraction

High-resolution powder diffraction for phase identification and in situ structural evolution of crystalline catalyst phases under reaction conditions.

🔬

Electron Microscopy

TEM and STEM imaging at the Center for Functional Nanomaterials (Brookhaven) to visualize catalyst morphology, particle size, and support interactions at the nanoscale.

📈

Kinetic Testing & Reactors

Steady-state and transient kinetic experiments including temperature-programmed surface reaction (TPSR), CH₄-pulsing, and continuous-flow packed-bed and membrane reactor evaluations.

⌨

Computational Methods

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations in collaboration with computational partners to complement experimental structure-activity data and elucidate reaction mechanisms.
