What We Do

Core Research Areas

From molecular-level catalyst design to industrial reactor engineering, our work spans the full spectrum of heterogeneous catalysis.

01

Methane Dehydroaromatization

Direct, one-step conversion of methane to benzene and hydrogen using zeolite-supported metal carbide catalysts — overcoming rapid coking and thermodynamic limits.

Learn more →
02

COx-free Hydrogen Production

Catalytic methane decomposition over novel materials to produce clean hydrogen with zero carbon oxide byproducts — a pathway to renewable energy carriers.

Learn more →
03

Membrane Reactor Engineering

Gradient-feed membrane reactors that distribute oxidant co-reactants to improve process thermodynamics, boost benzene selectivity, and prolong catalyst life.

Learn more →
04

In Situ & Operando Characterization

Advanced X-ray absorption (XAS) and high-resolution powder diffraction — performed at national synchrotron facilities — to capture catalyst evolution under real reaction conditions.

Learn more →
05

CO₂ Upgrading

Catalytic routes to convert CO₂ co-reactants into useful chemical feedstocks, supporting circular economy goals and greenhouse gas utilization.

Learn more →
06

Catalyst Stability & Deactivation

Understanding coke deposition, regeneration pathways, and structure-activity relationships to design catalysts with longer on-stream lifetimes.

Learn more →

New Funding · March 2025

$1.3M Grant to Build a Hydrogen Innovation Hub in Appalachia

Virginia Tech has received a $1.3 million grant from the Appalachian Regional Commission to establish a premier hydrogen innovation hub in Southwest Virginia. Led by Dr. Khatib, the project uses catalytic methane decomposition to produce turquoise hydrogen — clean hydrogen from natural gas with zero CO₂ emissions — while generating solid carbon byproducts with high-value applications in nanomaterials.

Partners: Asbury University · Shepherd Chemical Co. · VT Center for Rural Education
